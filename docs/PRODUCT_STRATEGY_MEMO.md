# Product Strategy Memo

## AI Code Quality Auditor

### Executive summary
AI Code Quality Auditor should be positioned as an AI assurance product for engineering organizations adopting agentic coding tools. The product’s value is not only to benchmark tools, but to give leadership a practical method for assessing trust, safety, and governance before adoption.

### Problem
Organizations are adopting AI coding tools quickly, but they lack a reliable way to evaluate whether these tools:
- remain aligned to business requirements
- produce secure, maintainable code
- avoid irrelevant or risky scope expansion
- meet internal governance standards

Existing benchmarking approaches rarely answer these questions. Many focus on whether code runs rather than whether it is safe, governable, and appropriate.

### Product thesis
The product should be framed as a decision-support system for AI-enabled software delivery.

Its core promise:
- evaluate AI coding tools against a fixed specification
- score outputs across security, complexity, duplication, hallucination, and interaction quality
- present results in a way that decision-makers can understand

### Target users
Primary users:
- engineering leaders
- AI adoption teams
- governance and risk stakeholders
- technology partners evaluating AI tooling

Secondary users:
- researchers and dissertation stakeholders
- technical teams running internal audits

### Product strategy pillars
1. User-first experience
   - The experience should feel clear, fast, and frictionless.
   - The product should make evidence understandable without requiring deep technical analysis.

2. Evidence-led assurance
   - The product should make assessment repeatable and auditable.
   - Results should be traceable to the underlying spec and metrics.

3. Enterprise relevance
   - The product should support procurement, policy, and adoption conversations.
   - It should help organizations move from experimentation to governed rollout.

### Product direction
The next phase should evolve the current prototype into three product layers:
- Evaluation engine: the core scoring and analysis workflow
- Executive dashboard: a simplified, decision-friendly interface
- Partner deployment layer: a repeatable path for pilots, demos, and enterprise validation

### Commercialization approach
The product can be positioned in three ways:
- as a governance tool for engineering teams
- as an assurance platform for AI adoption programs
- as a partner-facing evaluation layer for tool selection and risk screening

This supports multiple routes:
- self-serve adoption through CLI and dashboard
- enterprise pilots with custom evaluation criteria
- partner-led evaluation programs

### Roadmap priorities
Short term:
- sharpen the executive-facing dashboard
- simplify interpretation of results
- package materials for partner demos

Medium term:
- add reusable templates for enterprise evaluations
- support more guided workflows and onboarding
- expand the reporting layer for decision-makers

Long term:
- position the product as a broader AI assurance platform for software delivery

### Key message
The product should not be sold as a generic benchmark. It should be sold as a trust and governance layer for AI-assisted engineering: practical, repeatable, and designed for enterprise adoption decisions.

### Recommendation
Move forward with a partner-ready product narrative centered on assurance, governance, and decision support. The goal is not to create an impressive demo; the goal is to create a sellable product that helps organizations reduce risk and adopt AI coding tools with confidence.
